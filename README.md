# Good Life Study

Personal study project about how to live a good life.

This repo is meant to be broader than Stoicism alone. Stoicism remains an important input, but not the governing framework. The goal is to build an evolving synthesis from multiple sources: philosophy, psychology, habits, relationships, attention, work, health, meaning, technology, AI, and reflective practice.

## Purpose

This project is for:

- collecting source material worth preserving
- extracting principles and practices from that material
- organizing ideas by life domain rather than by author alone
- building a clearer personal model of what contributes to a good life

## Relationship To Other Repos

- `stoicism-personal-study/` remains a focused source repo and philosophical input
- `publishing/` is where ideas may first appear as posts, notes, or drafts
- `good-life-study/` is where durable ideas should be synthesized into a longer-term map

## Working Model

The core workflow is:

1. Capture a source under `sources/`.
2. Extract the important ideas or practices.
3. Map them into relevant domain notes under `domains/`, `principles/`, or `practices/`.
4. Update `synthesis/` when the new material changes the bigger picture.

The repo should not become a pile of summaries. The point is synthesis.

## Structure

- `principles/` - durable principles that seem repeatedly true across sources
- `domains/` - life areas such as attention, work, relationships, health, meaning, and technology/AI
- `practices/` - concrete exercises, habits, or behavioral patterns worth trying
- `sources/` - captured inputs from books, Blinkist, articles, and podcasts
- `synthesis/` - higher-level maps that connect ideas across domains
- `open-questions/` - unresolved tensions, uncertainties, and future lines of inquiry

## Initial Direction

The starting assumption is that a good life cannot be reduced to productivity or comfort. It likely depends on a combination of character, attention, relationships, meaning, and a healthy relationship with technology.
