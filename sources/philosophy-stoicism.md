# Stoicism

Source type: philosophical tradition

## Why this source matters

Stoicism offers useful ideas about character, judgment, agency, acceptance, and social responsibility. This note records those contributions as one source among others in the study.

## Ideas currently captured

### Character matters more than externals

Status, wealth, reputation, comfort, and circumstance are unstable. Treating character and the quality of one's choices as more important can make a life less dependent on conditions that cannot be secured.

### Judgment shapes experience

Events and our interpretation of them are not identical. Examining judgments can reduce avoidable suffering and improve action without requiring denial of pain or difficulty.

### Humans are social

Virtue is not only private self-management. Justice and right relationship with other people are part of living well.

### Acceptance and agency belong together

Acceptance concerns what cannot be changed; agency concerns the choices and actions still available. Either idea becomes distorted when separated from the other.

## Boundaries

These ideas should be evaluated on their usefulness and accuracy rather than treated as the governing framework for the project. Questions about relationships, attention, embodiment, psychology, technology, and environment should draw on whichever sources illuminate them best.
