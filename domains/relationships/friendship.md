# Friendship

Friendship is not only about having close contacts. It is a way of relating that combines presence, goodwill, and grounded care.

## Working principles

### Start with self-relationship

If someone relates to themselves mainly through criticism, shame, or performance, that pattern often leaks into friendship. A healthier friendship style starts with more self-compassion and less internal scorekeeping.

### Replace comparison with presence

Friendship weakens when relationships become competitive or transactional. Presence matters more than measuring who gives more, achieves more, or deserves more.

### Attention is a form of care

To give someone undivided attention is already meaningful. Listening carefully, noticing tone and body language, and resisting the urge to fix immediately are core friendship skills.

### Empathy needs boundaries

Strong friendship is neither emotional fusion nor distance. The healthier stance is to stay connected without losing oneself. Boundaries protect the relationship from resentment, burnout, and unclear responsibility.

### Care should become concrete

Friendship is sustained by follow-through: checking in, remembering details, being reliable, and acting on care rather than only feeling it.

## Practices worth testing

- during one conversation each day, give full attention without preparing your reply
- after someone shares something difficult, follow up later instead of treating the conversation as finished
- notice when comparison or hidden competition enters a relationship
- practice saying no clearly when needed, without aggression or apology spirals
- in moments of self-attack, speak to yourself as you would to a friend
